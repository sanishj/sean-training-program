01

Why this program

A middle blocker's job comes down to a handful of physical demands: a fast, repeatable vertical jump; quick lateral footwork to close blocks; a powerful arm swing; and the ability to do all of that over and over across a long match without your legs or shoulders breaking down. Since you're coming back from a lower training base, this plan is built in three stages so your body adapts in the right order — build a strength and landing-mechanics foundation first, then layer in real jump power, then peak toward match-specific explosiveness.

This plan runs alongside your volleyball schedule — Monday and Wednesday club practice are built in as your rest days from the gym.

02

How the 12 weeks are structured

Weeks 1–4

Phase 1 — Foundation

General strength, movement quality, and landing mechanics. Jump training stays low-impact: sticking landings, not maximal jumps.

Weeks 5–8

Phase 2 — Build

Strength loads increase. Plyometrics progress to moderate-intensity jumps with full recovery between reps.

Weeks 9–12

Phase 3 — Power

Heavier strength work paired with reactive, match-specific power: depth jumps, single-leg power, and blocking-footwork drills.

04

Warm-up — do this before every session

5–8 minutes, every single day, no exceptions.

  • 3–4 min easy cardio (bike, rower, or brisk treadmill walk)
  • Leg swings — front/back and side/side, 10 each leg
  • Bodyweight squats × 10, walking lunges × 8 each leg
  • Ankle pogo hops × 20 (small, quick, quiet bounces)
  • Arm circles + band or cable face pulls × 15

06

Cooldown — every session

5–10 minutes. Hamstring, hip flexor, chest/shoulder, and quad stretches, each held 30–45 seconds per side. Foam roll quads, calves, and upper back if available.

07

Plyometric safety rules

Read this before Day 1

  • Landings should be quiet. A loud slap or thud means you're not absorbing through your hips and knees — slow down and reset.
  • Full recovery between jump sets in Phases 2–3 — this is about quality, not fatigue.
  • Sharp joint pain, swelling, or pain that changes how you walk is a stop sign — get it looked at before continuing.
  • If you haven't had a sports physical recently, get one before starting, especially coming back from lower conditioning.
